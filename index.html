02 / WHO GETS OUTDOORS?

Who Gets Outdoors?

Overall participation appears remarkably balanced between genders. Yet similar participation does not mean identical preferences. Age shapes outdoor habits even further. These differences raise another question: does location matter?

KEY FINDING

Overall participation is remarkably balanced. Women represent 50.3% of participants. Men account for a similar 49.7%.

KEY FINDING

Walking shows the largest gender gap. Women record 57.8% participation in walking. Men record 42.5% participation in walking.

KEY FINDING

Outdoor habits shift with age. Running is strongest among younger Australians. Recreational walking becomes stronger with age. It peaks at 66.0% among ages 55–64.

06 / AUSTRALIA'S PROTECTED OUTDOORS

Protection Has Grown,
But Not Equally

Australia's protected landscapes have expanded over time. Historical maps reveal this changing national footprint. Yet growth has not been uniform across states. Outdoor activity also contributes beyond conservation.

KEY FINDING

Australia's protected footprint has expanded. CAPAD snapshots show increasingly extensive protected land. The change is visible from 2008 to 2024.

KEY FINDING

Western Australia recorded the largest increase. Coverage rose from 14.5% in 2008. It reached 31.1% in 2024. That is a 16.7 percentage-point increase.

07 / THE OUTDOORS AND THE ECONOMY

The Outdoors and
the Economy

Outdoor recreation contributes to Australia's tourism economy. The pandemic disrupted this economic activity. Tourism subsequently recovered strongly. But financial contributions tell only part of the story.

KEY FINDING

Sports and recreation tourism rebounded strongly. Output fell to $2.36 billion in 2020–21. It reached $5.06 billion in 2023–24.

KEY FINDING

Australia's wider tourism economy recovered strongly. Tourism GDP fell sharply during the pandemic. It reached $78.1 billion in 2023–24.
