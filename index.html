The Great Australian Outdoors

How participation, place and community shape Australia's outdoor life

Australia's outdoor culture extends beyond organised sport. From everyday activity to national parks and tourism, Australians connect with the outdoors in different ways. This story explores participation, motivation and place, alongside the systems supporting Australia's outdoor life.

01 / AUSTRALIA'S OUTDOOR LIFESTYLE

How Do Australians
Get Active?

Australians participate in many forms of physical activity. But participation is far from evenly distributed.

KEY FINDING

Walking leads the way. Recreational walking is by far the most popular activity, with 50.2% of Australian adults participating—well ahead of bush walking, running and swimming.

02 / WHO GETS OUTDOORS?

Who Gets Outdoors?

Overall participation is almost evenly split by gender. But activity choices reveal a different story.

KEY FINDING

Participation is almost evenly split by gender. Women represent 50.3% of Australian adult participants, while men account for a similar 49.7%.

KEY FINDING

Gender differences depend on the activity. Women participate more in recreational walking, while men record higher participation in running, cycling and golf. The largest gap appears in walking, at 57.8% for females compared with 42.5% for males.

KEY FINDING

Outdoor habits shift with age.
Running is strongest among younger Australians,
but declines substantially with age.
Recreational walking grows more prominent with age,
peaking at 66.0% among those aged 55–64.

04 / WHY DO AUSTRALIANS GET ACTIVE?

More Than
Just Exercise

Health and fitness may lead the motivations. But Australians are active for many different reasons.

KEY FINDING

Nature is a major reason to get active. More than half of Australian adults, 51.1%, cite being outdoors or enjoying nature.

06 / AUSTRALIA'S PROTECTED OUTDOORS

Protection Has Grown,
But Not Equally

Australia's protected landscape has expanded over time. But the scale of change differs across states and territories.

KEY FINDING

Australia's protected landscape has expanded over time.
Across the CAPAD snapshots, the terrestrial protected-area footprint
becomes visibly more extensive between 2008 and 2024.

KEY FINDING

Western Australia recorded the largest increase. Protected terrestrial coverage rose from 14.5% in 2008 to 31.1% in 2024, an increase of approximately 16.7 percentage points.

07 / THE ECONOMIC SIDE OF THE OUTDOORS

The Outdoors
Drives Activity

Outdoor recreation also contributes to Australia's tourism activity. Sports and recreation services reveal a strong post-pandemic recovery.

KEY FINDING

Sports and recreation tourism has rebounded strongly. Tourism output from these services fell to $2.36 billion in 2020–21, before climbing to $5.06 billion in 2023–24.

KEY FINDING

Australia's wider tourism economy has also recovered strongly. Tourism GDP reached $78.1 billion in 2023–24, following the sharp pandemic-era decline.

08 / MORE THAN JUST RECREATION

Behind Every Game,
People Make It Happen

Australia's active lifestyle depends on more than participants. Volunteers help keep sport and recreation running.

KEY FINDING

Around one in eight Australian adults volunteers in sport. In 2025, 12.1% participated in a non-playing volunteer role supporting sporting activity.

KEY FINDING

Officials and coaches are the most common volunteer roles. Around 3.8% of Australian adults volunteer as officials, while 3.6% contribute as coaches, instructors, trainers or teachers.

Adults may volunteer in more than one role, so role percentages are not mutually exclusive.
