The Great Australian Outdoors

How Australians move, explore, protect and contribute.

From everyday exercise to vast protected landscapes, outdoor life takes many forms across Australia. This story follows who participates and where. It explores why the outdoors matters. It also shows what the outdoors supports.

Australia Gets

Outdoor life begins with how Australians stay active. Yet one everyday activity clearly dominates participation. This begins the story of who gets active.

02 / WHO GETS OUTDOORS?

Who Gets Outdoors?

Overall participation looks remarkably balanced by gender. Look closer, and those similarities begin disappearing. Activity choices differ between men and women. Age reshapes participation even further.

KEY FINDING

Overall participation is remarkably balanced. Women represent 50.3% of participants. Men account for a similar 49.7%.

KEY FINDING

Walking shows the largest gender gap. Women record 57.8% participation in walking. Men record 42.5% participation in walking.

KEY FINDING

Outdoor habits shift with age.
Running is strongest among younger Australians.
Recreational walking becomes stronger with age.
It peaks at 66.0% among ages 55–64.

04 / WHY DO AUSTRALIANS GET ACTIVE?

More Than

Participation changes across people and places. But another question remains: why get active? Fitness matters, but it is not everything. Nature itself is part of the motivation.

Nature motivates more than half. 51.1% identify nature as a motivation. The environment itself encourages participation.

06 / AUSTRALIA'S PROTECTED OUTDOORS

Protection Has Grown,
But Not Equally

These wild places are not static landscapes. Australia's protected footprint has expanded over time. But that growth has been far from uniform. Some regions changed much more than others.

KEY FINDING

Australia's protected footprint has expanded.
CAPAD snapshots show increasingly extensive protected land.
The change is visible from 2008 to 2024.

KEY FINDING

Western Australia recorded the largest increase. Coverage rose from 14.5% in 2008. It reached 31.1% in 2024. That is a 16.7 percentage-point increase.

07 / THE OUTDOORS AND THE ECONOMY

The Outdoors and
the Economy

Outdoor life also intersects with Australia's economy. Sports and recreation contribute to tourism activity. After the pandemic, that activity recovered strongly. The wider tourism economy followed a similar rebound.

KEY FINDING

Sports and recreation tourism rebounded strongly. Output fell to $2.36 billion in 2020–21. It reached $5.06 billion in 2023–24.

KEY FINDING

Australia's wider tourism economy recovered strongly. Tourism GDP fell sharply during the pandemic. It reached $78.1 billion in 2023–24.

08 / MORE THAN JUST RECREATION

Behind Every Game,
People Make It Happen

The outdoors creates activity beyond participation itself. But money alone does not keep sport running. People do. Volunteers contribute across many essential roles.

KEY FINDING

12.1% of adults hold non-playing volunteer roles. These roles support sporting activity across Australia.

KEY FINDING

Officials are the most common volunteer role. 3.8% of Australian adults volunteer as officials. Coaching-related roles follow at 3.6%.

Adults may volunteer in more than one role. Role percentages are not mutually exclusive.
